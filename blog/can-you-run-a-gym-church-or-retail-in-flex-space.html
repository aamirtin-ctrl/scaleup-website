Can you run a gym, church, or retail store in flex space?

By the ScaleUp principals — 90+ combined years in construction, industrial operations & finance Published August 4, 2026 6 min read

Yes, you can run a gym, a church, or a retail store in flex space, as long as two things line up: the parcel allows the use, and the building can meet what that use asks of it. Most people check the first and forget the second, and the second is usually what decides whether you open. A gym, a church, and a store each put more demand on parking, restrooms, and exits than a plain warehouse does, and that is the part that catches tenants after they have signed.

If you have ever found a space you liked and then heard the words "the code won't let you do that here," you already know this is two questions wearing one coat.

Does zoning let you run a gym, church, or store in flex space?

It depends on the parcel: inside a city, a zoning map decides it, and on ETJ land there is no city zoning map, so the county rules and the park's recorded restrictions decide it instead. A gym, a fitness studio, and a showroom all read cleanly as flex uses in most light-industrial or commercial zones. A church or an event hall is an assembly use, and that is the one more likely to need a specific-use permit inside city limits. On land in a city's extraterritorial jurisdiction, that city zoning layer drops off entirely, which widens what you can run without filing for a rezoning. We cover that mechanism in full in what ETJ status means for a flex lease, and permitted use is item two on our flex space lease checklist.

What does the building itself need for an assembly or retail use?

The building code sorts uses into groups, and a gym or church is an assembly use, a store is a mercantile use, and most flex tenants are a business or storage use. Each group carries its own rules for how many exits you need, how many restroom fixtures, and whether the space has to be sprinklered. Move a use up into assembly or mercantile and those numbers climb, because you are now putting people in the room instead of pallets. That is the cost line generic "just check your zoning" advice skips.

How much parking does a gym or store need versus a warehouse?

A store or gym needs several times more parking per square foot than a warehouse, so parking, not the unit, is often the real constraint. A warehouse mostly holds inventory, so a city may ask for something like one stall per 1,000 square feet. Retail brings customers, and cities commonly want closer to one stall per 250 square feet. A gym can run higher still, because a class empties and fills the lot in waves. So a 3,000 SF bay that works fine for a distributor can come up short of stalls the moment you turn it into a storefront, and the fix is either fewer occupants or a park that was laid out with the parking to begin with.

How do you confirm your use before you sign?

Ask the landlord three plain questions before you commit: what does the parcel's zoning or recorded restriction allow for your use, what building code group does your use fall under, and does this specific unit already have the parking, restrooms, and exits that group needs. A developer who leases to gyms and stores on purpose will answer all three without hedging. North Texas is where these questions come up most, because the market is filling fast. Rockwall County grew 6.5% between July 2022 and July 2023, among the fastest of any large county in the nation, according to the U.S. Census Bureau. On land in a city's extraterritorial jurisdiction under Texas Local Government Code Chapter 42, the zoning question narrows to the county and the deed, which is one reason flex parks on that land can host a wider tenant mix.

The short version: zoning tells you whether the use is allowed, and the building code tells you whether the unit can carry it. A gym, a church, and a store all read as assembly or mercantile uses, which means more parking, more restrooms, and sometimes sprinklers than a warehouse. Confirm the use and the build-out together, before you sign.

FAQ

Can you run a gym or church in flex space?

Often yes, if the parcel's zoning or, on ETJ land, the county rules and recorded restrictions allow an assembly use, and the building can meet the parking, restroom, and exit requirements that a gym or church triggers. Confirm both the use and the build-out before you sign.

Why does a retail or gym use need more parking than a warehouse?

A store or gym brings customers, so cities require far more parking per square foot than a warehouse that mostly holds inventory. Retail commonly runs around one stall per 250 square feet and a gym can run higher, while warehouse use may need one per 1,000 square feet or more.

What building code classification applies to a gym, church, or store?

A gym or church is an assembly use, a store is a mercantile use, and most flex tenants are a business or storage use. Assembly and mercantile uses carry stricter rules for exits, restroom counts, and sprinklers, which is what the build-out has to satisfy.
