What size flex space does my business need?

By the ScaleUp principals — 90+ combined years in construction, industrial operations & finance Published June 12, 2026 Updated July 7, 2026 5 min read

Size a flex unit around three things: your crew, your inventory turn, and the biggest vehicle that loads there. Square footage follows from those, not the other way around. Here's the practical math we walk tenants through, using the three unit sizes we build at Rockwall Flex Park.

1,500 SF — the starter bay

Roughly a deep double garage plus an office. Fits a 2–4 person operation: wall racking down both sides, one van or box truck loading through the grade-level door, a modest office/reception up front. Right for service trades (plumbing, electrical, HVAC install), lean e-commerce, appliance or equipment repair, or a satellite location for an established company.

Outgrown when: you're staging tomorrow's jobs in the parking lot, or inventory has eaten your workbench.

2,000 SF — the workhorse

The most-requested size for a reason: adds a second working zone. Two vehicles can stage, or one vehicle plus a real assembly/prep area. Comfortable for 4–8 people. Wholesale distributors, sign shops, cabinet installers, gyms and studios, and growing e-commerce brands live here. Office front carries a couple of desks plus a customer counter.

3,000 SF — the showroom unit

At our parks the 3,000 SF units carry glass storefronts and highway visibility — built for businesses where customers see the product: flooring, outdoor kitchens, golf carts, furniture, building materials. Half display, half warehouse, with room for an office mezzanine conversation later. If foot traffic matters to your revenue, the visibility premium usually pays for itself.

The sizing math, quickly

  • People: ~150–200 SF per full-time person on site keeps an operation workable.
  • Inventory: count pallet positions or racking feet you use today, then add 30% — businesses that bother leasing space are usually growing into it.
  • Vehicles: a box truck needs ~35' of clear depth to pull in; if it lives inside overnight, budget its parking spot as floor area.
  • Customers: any walk-in traffic means the front 300–600 SF works as showroom/counter and can't double as storage.
When in doubt, size up. Stepping from 1,500 to 2,000 SF typically costs a few hundred dollars a month (see our DFW cost breakdown); moving your whole operation a year early costs weeks of disruption. If you're growing 20%+ annually, lease the bigger bay — small-business growth in North Texas has run hot for years (Dallas Fed Texas economic indicators).

FAQ

How much fits in a 1,500 SF flex unit?

A 2–4 person operation with one van or box truck loading daily, wall racking both sides, a small office up front, and working floor space in the middle.

Should I lease more space than I need right now?

If you're growing more than ~20% a year, yes. Moving costs real money and downtime; sizing up one unit costs a few hundred dollars a month.

Can I combine two flex units later?

Often yes — adjacent bays can be combined in parks designed for it. Ask about optioning adjacent space before you sign.
