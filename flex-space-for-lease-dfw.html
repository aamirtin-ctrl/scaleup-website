How to compare flex space developers in DFW

There are a number of small-bay parks going up across North Texas right now, and from a listing photo they look interchangeable. They are not.

We build these buildings, so here is what we would actually compare if we were the tenant — including the questions that expose a weak deal.

Total occupancy cost Ask for the base rate and the current NNN estimate and last year's actuals. A low headline rate with vague NNNs often costs more by year two.
Delivered condition in writing Office finish, restroom, HVAC tonnage, lighting, slab. Anything not listed in the lease exhibit becomes your bill after move-in.
Power and phase Look at the panel, not the brochure. Adding 3-phase after the fact costs five figures.
Door size and truck approach A 12×12 handles a box truck. Then walk the approach — can your truck actually line up, or does a parking row block the angle?
Permitted use for your business Your use has to be allowed twice: by the lease clause and by zoning. Gyms, retail, assembly, and auto work are the common trip-ups.
Who actually operates the park Ask whether the developer manages the property or hands it to a third party. It determines who answers when the HVAC fails in August.
The four questions

What is the quoted base rate? What are estimated NNNs this year? What did NNNs actually run last year? What buildout is included in the delivered unit? Any landlord who cannot answer all four in one email is telling you something.

The long version, with everything that belongs on a walkthrough, is our 10-point flex space lease checklist.

What flex space costs in North Texas

Small-bay flex in outer DFW submarkets generally quotes in the mid-teens per square foot per year, triple-net, plus NNN charges.

That is not a premium for nothing. Small bays cost more to build and operate per square foot: more walls, doors, restrooms, HVAC units, and storefront glass per 1,000 SF, and more tenants to manage. Scarcity does the rest — nationally, small-bay represents a very small share of new industrial construction while demand from small businesses keeps climbing.

Choosing your unit size

Most first-time flex tenants land between 1,500 and 3,000 SF. Size around three things, in this order:

  • People — roughly 150 to 200 SF per full-time person on site keeps an operation workable.
  • Inventory — count the racking feet or pallet positions you use today, then add about 30 percent. Businesses that bother leasing space are usually growing into it.
  • Vehicles — a box truck needs roughly 35 feet of clear depth to pull in. If it lives inside overnight, that is floor area you cannot use for anything else.

Stepping up one size typically costs a few hundred dollars a month; moving your whole operation a year early costs weeks of disruption. The detailed breakdown is in what size flex space your business needs.

Common questions

How do I compare flex space developers in DFW?

Compare on six things rather than rate alone: total occupancy cost including NNN, delivered condition in writing, power and phase, door size and truck approach, permitted use for your specific business, and whether the landlord operates the park themselves. A low base rate with vague NNN estimates usually costs more in year two.

What does flex space cost to lease in DFW?

Generally mid-teens per square foot per year triple-net in outer submarkets, plus NNN charges. Small bays cost more per square foot than big-box warehouse because of finished office, glass, and individual HVAC, but total monthly cost is far lower because you lease a fraction of the footage.

What size flex unit do most small businesses need?

Most first-time tenants land between 1,500 and 3,000 SF. Size around roughly 150 to 200 SF per person on site, your current racking plus about 30 percent, and the largest vehicle that will load there.
